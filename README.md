# Phishing Awareness & Cyber Hygiene Training

This repository contains the training presentation, curriculum outline, and awareness materials developed for **TASK 2: Phishing Awareness Training** under the **CodeAlpha Cyber Security Internship Program**.

---

## 📌 Project Overview
Phishing and social engineering attacks remain among the primary attack vectors threatening organizational assets. This project aims to educate users, employees, and students on identifying phishing indicators, analyzing psychological manipulation tactics, and adhering to cyber hygiene best practices to safeguard sensitive credentials and digital environments.

---

## 🎯 Key Learning Objectives
- **Phishing Categorization:** Understanding common vectors including Email Phishing, Spear Phishing, Smishing, Vishing, and Clone Phishing.
- **Psychological Drivers:** Analyzing social engineering triggers such as manufactured urgency, authority impersonation, intimidation, and reward exploitation.
- **Red Flag Recognition:** Spotting mismatched domains, fraudulent headers, disguised hyperlinks, and dangerous attachment formats.
- **Fake Portal Identification:** Demystifying SSL/HTTPS padlock assumptions, typosquatting domains, and cloned SSO interfaces.
- **Defense Protocols:** Establishing multi-factor authentication (MFA), password manager integration, and out-of-band communication checks.

---

## 📑 Presentation Structure
1. **Title & Introduction:** Overview of Cyber Hygiene Training
2. **Phishing Foundations:** Core attack vectors and delivery mechanisms
3. **Social Engineering Psychology:** Manipulation triggers exploited by threat actors
4. **Email Red Flags:** Dissecting malicious emails and obfuscated links
5. **Deceptive Portals & Domains:** Typosquatting and fake corporate login interfaces
6. **Real-World Breach Analysis:** Step-by-step SSO credential harvesting workflow
7. **Mitigation & Hygiene:** Practical security countermeasures
8. **Interactive Assessment:** Scenario-based knowledge check
9. **Conclusion & Core Takeaways:** Building a resilient security culture
